01 / Overview

An early experiment that became part of the journey.

Raremode began as an independent web project and an opportunity to experiment with digital design, fashion branding, product presentation, and frontend development.

Instead of presenting the old project as though it were recently built, this portfolio version documents the idea and reimagines its presentation with today's design standards. It gives the project a place in my development story while keeping the original spirit of experimentation.

02 / What I Built

A digital experience designed around clarity.

The reconstructed concept focuses on the fashion-commerce experience behind Raremode: showcasing art-designed jean jackets while keeping the interface clean, visual, and easy to explore.

01

Responsive layout

A flexible interface designed to adapt across desktop, tablet, and mobile screens.

02

Product showcase

Large visual compositions give the art-designed jean jackets a strong presence and make the products easy to explore.

03

Interactive details

Subtle hover states, navigation behavior, and scroll interactions add personality without distraction.

04

Fashion identity

The interface connects denim, artwork, and brand identity into one distinctive visual experience.

03 / Design Direction

Dark. Editorial.
Distinct.

The visual direction uses a restrained dark palette, warm metallic accents, oversized typography, generous spacing, and product-inspired compositions. The goal is a premium feeling without making the interface difficult to use.

06 / Lessons Learned

Every early project leaves something behind.

Raremode represents an important stage of learning: taking an idea and turning it into something people can actually experience on the web. Looking back at an early project also makes progress visible — from simply making a website work to thinking about structure, usability, performance, accessibility, visual identity, and maintainable code.

“The value of an early project isn't only what you built. It's what it taught you to build next.”
